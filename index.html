A supercomputer is only fast if all of its processors stay busy. I build the software that keeps them busy, and that makes them easier to program.

I’m an Associate Professor and Chair of Computer Science at Rhodes College in Memphis, Tennessee. My research is in parallel and high-performance computing (HPC): programming models and runtime systems for irregular applications, and lately, new ways to use the network hardware itself to balance work across a machine. I do most of this work with Rhodes undergraduates. I also led the effort to bring HPC to Rhodes. lotus, the NSF-funded cluster I brought to Briggs Hall, is now a shared research computing resource for the whole campus. More about my research →

In the classroom, I teach much of the systems curriculum, along with parallel computing, compilers, and study-abroad courses in the United Kingdom and Japan. I teach because I’m passionate about learning, and in 2021 I received Rhodes College’s Clarence Day Award for Outstanding Teaching. More about my teaching →

Before Rhodes, I taught at Coastal Carolina University, earned my Ph.D. at Ohio State, and spent a decade in industry at Bell Labs and two startups. Outside of work, I’m into photography, reef keeping, cooking, and coffee. Here are some of my photos.
