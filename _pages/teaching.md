I have taught at every level of the undergraduate computer science curriculum,
at Rhodes College, Coastal Carolina University, Capital University, and The Ohio
State University. I teach most of the computer systems sequence at Rhodes, and I
like to give students real-world problems to solve, including assignments that run
on the [Rhodes HPC cluster]({{ '/research/#research-computing-at-rhodes' | relative_url }})
and on national supercomputers. In 2021, I received Rhodes College's
[Clarence Day Award for Outstanding Teaching](https://news.rhodes.edu/stories/profs-larkins-and-newstok-receive-2021-clarence-day-awards).

## Computer systems

**COMP 231: Introduction to Computer Organization.**
A bottom-up exploration of the interaction between computer hardware and software:
information representation, elementary processor design, assembly language,
instruction encoding, and computer arithmetic. Labs use FPGA development boards.

**COMP 251: Introduction to Computer Systems.**
An introduction to system-aware programming. Students learn how computer
architecture, operating systems, compilers, and networks affect the performance and
behavior of programs, and they learn a systems programming language and tools.
Topics include memory management, caching and the memory hierarchy, system calls,
network programming, processes, threads and concurrency, and performance analysis.

**COMP 315: Computer Networking.**
The structure and components of computer networks: packet switching, layered
architectures, network services and protocols, router architectures, network-aware
applications, content distribution, and network security.

**COMP 330: Operating Systems.**
Processes, scheduling, context switching, inter-process communication,
synchronization, memory management, and device drivers. Students study Unix/Linux
kernel design and implement their assignments in C.

## Parallel and high-performance computing

**COMP 380: Parallel Systems.**
An introduction to concurrent programming models and parallel computer
architectures. Students learn parallel program design, implementation, and
performance analysis, using shared-memory (OpenMP) and distributed-memory (MPI,
OpenSHMEM) programming on the Rhodes HPC cluster.

## Languages and theory

**COMP 350: Theory of Computation.**
Finite automata, regular languages, context-free grammars and pushdown automata,
Turing machines, decidability, complexity theory, and NP-completeness.

**COMP 360: Programming Languages.**
Fundamental programming language concepts, the design issues behind language
constructs, and how a range of contemporary languages resolve them, along with
syntax description, semantics, and implementation approaches.

**COMP 362: Compiler Construction.**
Lexical analysis, parsing, syntax tree generation and traversal, code generation,
and optimization. Students build a complete compiler in several phases over the
semester.

## Other courses

**COMP 142: Computer Science II.** Object-oriented programming, data abstraction,
algorithm analysis, and searching and sorting.

**COMP 340: Databases.** Data models, relational algebra, database design, and
query languages.

## Study abroad

**COMP 105: Transformational Ideas in Computing from the United Kingdom** (Summer 2024).
A three-week course on the foundations of computing, in both theory and early
implementations. It focuses on the contributions of Alan Turing and John von
Neumann. Students visit Milton Keynes, Cambridge, London, and Edinburgh to see
early mechanical and electronic machines such as Babbage's Difference Engine, the
Jacquard loom, and the Bombe and Colossus computers.

**COMP 106: Robotics in Japan** (May 19 – June 20, 2027).
A course on robotics seen through Japanese history and culture, from the
mechanical automata of the Edo period to today's humanoid robots. Students learn
the fundamentals of mobile robotics (sensors, obstacle avoidance, planning, and
navigation) and program robots, with a home base in Tokyo and excursions to Kyoto,
Nagoya, and Osaka.

## K–12 outreach

I am committed to building interest and confidence in computing among K–12
students. I led Camp Codette, a summer
programming camp for middle and high school girls at Rhodes (2016–2019). Before
that, I ran the CCU Robocamp and mentored FIRST LEGO League and FIRST Robotics
Competition teams in South Carolina. Before graduate school, I spent eight years
teaching computer science to Columbus-area high school students through an AT&T
Bell Laboratories Explorer post.
